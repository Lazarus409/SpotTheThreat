Field guide

Six questions to ask before you act

Not sure about any of these? Stop and check first.

01 · Who

Is it really them?

Check the full email address, not just the name. Names and caller ID can be faked.

02 · Pressure

Am I being rushed?

Urgent deadlines and threats are meant to stop you thinking.

03 · Request

What do they want?

Passwords, codes, PINs or money? Stop and check.

04 · Links

Where does the link go?

Hover before you click. Is the web address the real one?

05 · Attachments

Was I expecting this file?

If not, don't open it.

06 · Channel

Can I check another way?

Call a number you already know, not the one in the message.

WorkMIIF work email (Microsoft 365)

  1. Install Microsoft Authenticator on your phone from the App Store or Google Play.
  2. On your computer, go to aka.ms/mfasetup and sign in with your work account.
  3. Choose Add sign-in method → Microsoft Authenticator → Next.
  4. In the app, tap + → Work or school account → Scan QR code, and scan the code on screen.
  5. Approve the test notification by typing the number shown on your computer.

EmailGmail / Google account

  1. Go to myaccount.google.com → Security.
  2. Under how you sign in to Google, choose 2-Step Verification → Turn on.
  3. Add a second step: Google prompts on your phone, an Authenticator app, or a passkey.
  4. Open Backup codes, then print or save them somewhere safe and offline.

MobileWhatsApp

  1. Open WhatsApp → Settings → Account.
  2. Tap Two-step verification → Turn on.
  3. Choose a 6-digit PIN that isn't your MoMo or bank PIN.
  4. Add an email address so you can reset the PIN if you forget it.

This stops the "code by mistake" takeover, because a stolen SMS code alone isn't enough.
